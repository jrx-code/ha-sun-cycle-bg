Sun Cycle Background — day simulator

The exact palette and placement the card runs on your dashboard, driven here by real astronomy instead of sun.sun. The sun follows its true diurnal arc for the chosen latitude and date, the moon runs on its own ephemeris with its real phase, and the sky colors interpolate continuously — dawn, golden hour, dusk and night simply fall out of the sun's elevation.

24 h simulator

Drag the sliders or press play (full day in 60 s). The sun rises in the east, culminates in the south and sets in the west along a path tilted by (90° − latitude); crepuscular rays fan out near the horizon and fade as it climbs. The moon keeps its own schedule and phase. Stars drift east to west (sped up here ~20×; the card defaults to 30 min per screen width).

Palette anchors

Between anchors everything is linear RGB interpolation — steps are invisible, because solar elevation moves by fractions of a degree per minute.

Tuning: the palette lives in the STOPS table (same values here and in sun-cycle-bg.js): seven anchors keyed by elevation, each with sky RGB, the sun halo and star opacity. Positions are not in the table — sun and moon are placed from their real coordinates, so latitude and date change the arc on their own.
